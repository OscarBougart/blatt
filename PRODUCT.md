# Product

## Register

product

## Users

One intermediate German learner (roughly B1 to B2), reading on a phone, one
handed, often in short sittings: on a commute, in bed, in a queue. They can get
through a German paragraph with effort and tend to reach for the translation
too early. Blatt is also a public portfolio piece, so a second audience is
anyone judging the craft of the build.

## Product Purpose

Read German texts with a hidden English translation, and turn the words saved
along the way into spaced-repetition flashcards. Local first: no account, no
server, works offline. Success is a reader who stays in German longer, and a
flip count that falls over time.

## Brand Personality

Quiet, bookish, exacting. The voice is a well-made printed page, not an app
trying to motivate you. German is ink, English is graphite: the same voice
written fainter, like a pencil gloss in a margin. Restraint is the aesthetic.
The emotional goal is calm focus, with a slight, honest discomfort when the
reader flips to English.

## Anti-references

- Gamified language apps: streaks, XP, badges, levels, mascots, confetti.
- Side-by-side bilingual readers where both languages are visible at once.
- Dashboard chrome while reading: toolbars, progress bars, floating buttons.
- Decorative texture: paper grain, noise, drop shadows, gradients.
- Pure black dark modes.

## Design Principles

1. **German is the default; English costs a deliberate gesture.** Any design
   choice that makes English easier to reach, or visible alongside German, is
   wrong even if it is more convenient.
2. **The text is the interface.** While reading, nothing competes with the
   column. Chrome exists only off the reading screen.
3. **One honest signal.** A single signal colour, used for the one statistic
   the app keeps: how often you flipped to English. Colour is information,
   not decoration.
4. **Readable in a year.** Visual rules stay few and explicit so the system can
   be understood from the tokens alone.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA: 4.5:1 for body text, 3:1 for large text and for UI boundaries
that carry meaning. Touch targets at least 48 by 48 px. Text size is user
adjustable (16 / 19 / 22 px) because pinch zoom is disabled on the reading
view. Keyboard access with visible focus. The off-screen language pane is inert
and hidden from assistive technology.
