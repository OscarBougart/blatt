## App name (30 max)

Blatt

## Short description (80 max)

Read German. Peek at English only when you mean to.

## Full description (4000 max)

Reading in a foreign language usually fails the same way. The translation sits
in the next column, or a tap away, and within a page you are reading English
and telling yourself you are practising German.

Blatt makes English cost something.

German and English are never on screen at the same time. You read the German
paragraph. If you are genuinely stuck, you flip it over, and the same paragraph
is there in English — same size, same line height, same shape of column, just
written fainter, the way a pencil gloss sits in a margin. Then you flip back.
The flip is deliberate, and you notice yourself doing it. That is the whole
design.


WHAT IT DOES

Read. Short stories and articles, one paragraph at a time, in a clean column
with nothing else on the screen. No toolbars, no progress bar, no chrome of any
kind while you are reading.

Flip. Any paragraph turns over to English and back. Never both at once.

Save. Tap a word and it is saved with the full sentence it came from, and a
definition looked up for you. It remembers the word as it appeared in the text
and the dictionary form underneath it, so "gegangen" and "ging" both find
"gehen".

Review. Saved words come back as spaced-repetition flashcards, scheduled with
SM-2. Cards show the sentence with the word blanked out, so you are recalling
it in context rather than staring at a list. Rounds of 5, 10 or 20.

Import. Bring your own text. Paste it, or share a file to Blatt from anywhere
on your phone.


ONE HONEST STATISTIC

No streaks. No XP. No badges, levels or achievements. Blatt tracks exactly one
number: your flip rate — how often you needed the English. It is the only
measure that tells you anything true, and it is meant to be slightly
uncomfortable.


BUILT SMALL AND LOCAL

Everything lives on your phone. No account, no sign-up, no cloud, no sync, no
server holding your reading. It works fully offline, including the bundled
library, so it is the same app on a plane as on wifi. The only thing that ever
leaves your device is a single word sent to Wiktionary when a definition is
looked up, and even that is cached so it never happens twice.

Comes with twelve of Grimm's fairy tales, translated and ready to read.

Dark mode is warm, not black. Text size is adjustable. Built for one-handed
reading on a phone.


WHO IT IS FOR

Intermediate learners — roughly B1 to B2 — who can get through a German
paragraph with effort and keep reaching for the translation too early. If you
are a beginner, Blatt will be frustrating: it gives you no vocabulary lists, no
grammar explanations and no conjugation tables, on purpose.
